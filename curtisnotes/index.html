Before you write

Check your first idea. Too general? Too specific? A key point should fit one body paragraph and one Hong Kong example.

  • Too general — “It helps education.”
  • Too specific — one shop name with no wider point
  • About right — golf (cost); theme parks (paid hangouts); workshops (heritage); government fund (policy)

Do not hesitate too long. Say the first idea, then adjust. A vivid example can suggest a bigger point, or a key point can lead to a lower-level example.

Part A — Nomination statement

Write about 200 words on the following topic.

You are running for the position of House Captain or Student Union Chairperson. Write a nomination statement to be posted on the school notice board.

Include a heading and use these two subheadings:

  • Past Contributions and Experience
  • Commitment to the Student Body

Step 1 — Qualities of a good house leader

Think about these first. Then pick experiences that prove them.

  • Responsible · confident · charming · athletic · know-how · communicative

Step 2 — Shape

  • Greeting / intro — My name is … form … house … post you want
  • Past Contributions and Experience — roles across house life, not only one sport; one vivid story
  • Commitment to the Student Body — two concrete plans if elected

Tone. Notice board, but you are speaking to housemates — warm and direct, not a formal essay.

Language — vary sentence openings

SVO is fine. A is B is fine. But do not start every sentence the same way.

With … With 2 years of experience in the cheering team and being the cheering leader last year, I am confident in coordinating our cheering practice properly with the best facilities and schedule to make sure we have the greatest chance to win the award this year. Having been … Having been in the school basketball team for the last three years, I know well enough about the best players from other houses, including their strengths and weaknesses. As the house leader, I will establish the best strategies to crush their team this year. As many of you may have seen … As many of you may have seen me on the school campus TV channel, I am tech-savvy with excellent aesthetic senses. I know the best artists from the campus TV station who will come and help us promote our house events this year if I am to become the house leader! Our promotion materials and souvenirs will be top-notch!

Draft notes (Chris Wong, Love House)

  • Opening can be tighter — name, form, nomination, one line on why you are running
  • Past section — helper, captain, cheering leader, basketball/volleyball are good; keep a broader range, not only the basket story
  • Basketball story (80–81, three seconds) — strong; keep it brief
  • Commitment — avoid only “I am fit for this”; say what you will do (cheering schedule, junior support, house events)

Part B — Traditional shops proposal

You are the Chairperson of the Community Heritage Club. Many traditional family-run shops in your area are closing. Write a proposal to the District Council.

  • Explain why these shops are important; and
  • Suggest two practical ways to encourage more young people to visit and support these local businesses.

Why they matter (from draft)

  • Heritage and family memory — skills hard to find today
  • Livelihood — owners trained for years; few other job options if the shop closes
  • Local area — e.g. Happy Baking (since 1980) replaced by a claw-machine shop; high rent, fewer visitors
  • Tourism potential — unique shops can attract visitors and stay overnight

Link suggestions to young people (SHEFFFFF)

Every suggestion must connect to why young people would visit. Do not stop at outcomes like “learn history”.

Fun

  • Workshops / DIY in the shop — bake, craft, print
  • KOL + IG reels + prizes / competitions
  • Social time with friends or family (e.g. Mother’s Day session)

Education

  • School heritage tours — teachers sign up; class visits the shop
  • Summer internships — DSE students as guides, designers, event helpers (subsidised)

Safety / environment

  • Revitalise old buildings — stable structure, better hygiene
  • Clean, safe spaces where young people want to visit and take photos

Financial (supports owners — link to youth where you can)

  • Short-term rent aid after COVID; tax allowance; crossover with artists for souvenirs
  • Social enterprise model — café, small market, exhibition space

Two suggestions for the proposal

  1. Social media + workshops — reels, KOL visits, hands-on sessions that bring young people in
  2. School programmes + internships — class tours and paid summer roles tied to promotion work

Government funding alone does not answer “encourage young people to visit.” Pair money with a youth-facing activity.

2. Letter — free hangout spaces

You are a member of the Youth Council. Recently, many teenagers have complained that there are few free places to hang out with friends outside of school and home. Write a letter to the editor of the Hong Kong Daily.

( What are the reasons for that? Who’s responsible? Who can help? )

Related angle from class:

  • Discuss how the lack of free social spaces affects the well-being of young people; and
  • Suggest how the government and non-government organisations can do more to help with the situation.

Brainstorm both sides

  • Yeah — we don’t have much free space to hang out
  • Na — we do have somewhere to go (but often it costs money)
  • Financial pressure — restaurants, cafes
  • Paid options — theme parks, arcade centres, shopping malls, cinemas

Plan the letter

  • Reasons — cost; few free youth-friendly spaces; homework leaves little time
  • Well-being — boredom, isolation, always at home or online
  • Who can help — government (parks, seating, evening access); NGOs (after-school hubs); schools (safe spaces after class)

3. Speech — ECAs and the SLP

Many secondary students in Hong Kong feel that their extra-curricular activities are no longer for fun, but are treated as a way to “boost” their Student Learning Profiles (SLP) for university applications. Write a speech to be delivered at a school assembly.

  • Discuss the drawbacks of treating these activities merely as a means to an end; and
  • Encourage your fellow students to choose activities based on personal fulfilment and genuine interest rather than just exam pressure. (Black hats + blue green hats on SHEFFFFF)

Examples — use one or two, not a bare list

Violin, taekwondo, erhu, astronaut programme, golf — pick one, explain it. Too many names with no detail looks like a list, not an argument.

Black hat — drawbacks (against treating ECAs as SLP tools)

  • Financial cost — golf, graded exams, expensive classes; money wasted if you do not enjoy it
  • Education inefficiency — join for the certificate, not the skill; little real practice
  • Future — skills not developed if you quit after getting the paper
  • Well-being — stress, comparison, burnout; activities stop being fun

Encourage — genuine interest

  • Choose what you would still do without university applications
  • Depth beats quantity — one real passion beats five empty SLP lines

4. Article — slow living

In a fast-paced city, some young people are embracing “slow living”, a lifestyle that prioritises mindfulness over speed and efficiency. Write an article for a lifestyle magazine.

  • Explain why the “slow living” movement is becoming popular among the youth; and
  • (What are the reasons for that? Who’s responsible? Who can help? )
  • Discuss whether it is actually possible for a student to “slow down” while managing heavy workloads and the pressure to succeed in Hong Kong. (Black hats+blue green hats on SHEFFFFF, context )

FOR — why it is popular

  • Mental health / relaxation — enjoy the moment; less anxiety
  • Reaction to a fast city — tired of always rushing
  • Learning — slow down to deepen understanding of skills and topics
  • True relaxation — fishing, meditation, one quiet café

AGAINST / nuance — can HK students slow down?

Contextualise inefficiency — do not stay abstract.

  • Past-paper time limits in exams
  • SBA project deadlines
  • Tight revision and quiz schedules
  • Frequent / daily homework
  • Pressure to succeed — DSE, university, career

Irony — slow living with friends

Even a “relaxing” day out can become a hectic chain:

Dining → karaoke → ball games → arcade → war game → desserts → exhausted.

Contrast with one slow activity. Conclusion — full slow living may be unrealistic; small slow habits may still help.
