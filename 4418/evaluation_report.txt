  - South China Morning Post (2023): Hong Kong–specific (overwork, hours).
  - No other references are Hong Kong–specific. Remaining refs are international (workplace well-being, JD–R, chatbots, measures, ethics).

ASSESSMENT
  - Strength: The study explicitly frames Hong Kong as the context and uses two local sources for stress/overwork. The research gap is clearly tied to "high-pressure contexts such as Hong Kong."
  - Limitation: Hong Kong appears in only two places in the main text. There is no mention of:
    - Hong Kong labour market, sector mix, or EAP/wellness adoption.
    - Local regulations or norms regarding workplace mental health or data privacy.
    - Any Hong Kong–based studies on AI, chatbots, or employee wellbeing (if available).
  - For a 600–1,000 word progress report, the current HK emphasis is acceptable but could be strengthened in a full report or in a Version 3 if the goal is to maximise local relevance now.

WHICH VERSION RELATES TO HONG KONG MORE?
  - Neither V1 nor V2 differs in Hong Kong content. The amount and type of Hong Kong context are the same. The only difference is that V2 adds more international workplace/implementation citations (Jackson-Triche, Dutta & Mishra, Baek); these do not reduce HK relevance but do not add HK-specific evidence either.
