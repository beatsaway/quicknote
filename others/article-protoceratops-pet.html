School magazine article — which dinosaur would you keep as a pet?

Meet the dinosaurs (Six Hats). Are chickens dinosaurs? Some dinosaurs (and pterosaurs) would make terrible pets. Size, food, danger and space all matter. Birds are living dinosaur relatives — but that does not mean a T-rex belongs in a flat.

Task. Write an article for your school magazine. If you could keep one as a pet — a pterosaur, a Triceratops, a Dreadnoughtus, a T-rex, or another kind — which would you choose? Give three reasons.

  • Success criteria: clear choice; 3 reasons; introduction and conclusion.
  • Only short yellow phrases are suggested edits; the rest is the draft as written.
  • Click flow. Yellow → green + comment. Only one comment at a time.
Useful words pet, space / flat, feed / diet, dangerous, trainable, responsibility, cost, neighbour, chicken / bird, descendant, realistic, choose, First of all, Secondly, Thirdly, In conclusion

What went well

Clear choice + three practical reasons — strong magazine plan.

  • 🐔 Hook. Chickens as living dinosaur relatives opens the topic well.
  • 🦖 Smart contrast. T-rex / Dreadnoughtus / pterosaur show why size and danger matter.
  • 🌱 Plant-eater detail. Lawnmower + leftover salad makes the pet idea feel realistic.

Could be even better

  • 💡 Linkers. First of all / Secondly / Thirdly / In conclusion.
  • 💡 Fragments & incomplete ideas. Need a large park to move; but no heavy damage.
  • 💡 Articles & commas. with dinosaur style; comma before I would.
  • 💡 everyday vs every day. every day = each day; everyday clothes = adjective.

The Ultimate Prehistoric Companion: Why a Protoceratops Belongs in Your Backyard

Chickens show us everyday that living dinosaur relatives still walk around.. Bringing a real ancient animal into a suburban neighborhood is hard. A T‑rex would turn the street into a buffet. A Dreadnoughtus would not fit on a football field. A huge pterosaur would cut power lines. If I could bring one animal back to live with me I would choose the Protoceratops. The Protoceratops is a sheep‑sized cousin of the well‑known Triceratops.

First the Protoceratops is small enough for a home. It is six feet long and about knee‑high. That size is similar to a dog. The Protoceratops will not break floorboards. Need a large park to move. Better the Protoceratops only eats plants. You will not need a truck of meat to feed it. The Protoceratops will eat lawn clippings, garden weeds and leftover salad. It can act like a lawnmower.

Second the Protoceratops acts like a guard dog but with dinosaur style. Its strong neck frill and sharp beak look fierce. The Protoceratops is safe for the family. The tough beak and frill would stop porch thieves and pests. This gives the home a strong pet that has the majestic look of a Triceratops but no heavy damage.

Third the Protoceratops is a low‑maintenance, backyard companion. High-strung mammals shed fur on furniture. Predators need a lot of training. The Protoceratops only needs sunshine, a shelter and plants. Watching the Protoceratops bask, on patio bricks or dig nests in the garden turns the home into a living science lab.

Giant carnivores belong in movies. Huge sauropods belong in parks. The small Protoceratops balances wonder and everyday practicality. The Protoceratops brings the Mesozoic Era to the back porch. It shows that the right choice can keep a pet safe.
