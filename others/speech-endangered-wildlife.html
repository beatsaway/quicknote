School assembly speech about endangered wildlife

You are giving a speech at a school assembly about endangered wildlife. Explain why these animals are disappearing, give at least two reasons to protect them, and finish with a clear call to action. Your audience is the Principal, your teachers and your schoolmates, so keep the sentences short enough to say aloud.

  • The audience is a whole-school assembly. The tone is personal, honest and persuasive.

What went well

It has the shape of a real assembly speech. A greeting, a question to the audience, reasons, and a clear ask at the end.

  • 🎯 Task fit. Greeting, examples (sharks, tigers, elephants, crocodiles), two reasons, and a call to action. Everything a school speech needs is here.
  • ❤️ Honesty. Saying the problem is “us” takes courage and makes listeners lean in. The crocodile bags and shark-fin soup are concrete, not abstract.
  • 🔗 Cause and effect. The chain from buyers to hunters to money is easy to follow, and the shark and seaweed example shows why one missing species matters.
  • 📏 Length. About 397 words is roughly three minutes at speaking pace. That suits an assembly slot.

Could be even better

  • 💡 Spoken sentences. Break the long chains into short sentences. Several places are fragments or run-ons, and those are hard to say aloud in front of a hall.
  • 💡 Word forms. Extinct is an adjective and extinction is the noun. Extincted is not a word. Use fewer with countable things (fewer animals) and less with uncountable ones (less demand).
  • 💡 Facts. The dodo died out in the 17th century, not the 18th. For the “four or five species a day” claim, add scientists estimate so the number sounds researched rather than guessed.
  • 💡 Repetition. “Do you know that” opens two paragraphs in a row. Vary the second hook.
