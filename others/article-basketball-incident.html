What went well

A clear problem, rising worry, and a satisfying turn when the neighbour values honesty.

  • 📖 Plot — setup (playground game), accident (window), fear, confrontation, and resolution read in order.
  • ✨ Character moment — Peter owns up; the neighbour’s lesson about honesty lands as the moral.
  • 💬 Dialogue — the owner’s angry question adds drama; Susan’s offer to help shows care.
  • 🔚 Ending — Nancy’s “meaningful day” closes the recount with reflection.

Could be even better

  • 💡 Word choice: “luckiest day” clashes with fear—match the mood (e.g. worst / unluckiest) or explain irony if you mean it.
  • 💡 Grammar: watch subject–verb agreement (“reason … is that she was dishonest”) and articles (a / the).
  • 💡 Spelling: everything is one word; neighbour’s needs an apostrophe for possession.
  • 💡 Idioms: stood out usually means “was noticeable”—stepped forward fits confessing.
  • 💡 Broken sentence: fix the line about Peter running away so readers know what he said and did.

A Basketball Incident

Last Tuesday after school, Nancy, Susan and Peter were playing basketball happily in a playground near Nancy’s house. They were having a competition when the girl's team was winning the competition. Peter became more excited and threw the ball not knowing an incident was coming.

Nancy and Susan didn’t manage to stop the ball but it hit Nancy's neighbours window. Nancy was frightened because her neighbour is very strict and she might get punished. Susan and Peter screamed loudly, not willing to get hurt by the glass. Everyone thought it will be the luckiest day of their life.

Peter just ran swiftly away with a and told the girls he was going to be shocked and worried face doomed. Meanwhile Nancy and Susan were thinking how to apologize to the owner. Every thing got messy at that time.

There were heavy footsteps coming by. When the owner came to the broken window, he angrily asked “Who did this? Tell me now.” The children stood like a stick. Nobody wanted to get punished. But just then, Peter stood out and told the owner he was the one who broke the glass. However, the owner didn’t scold him, but told him he was an honest child. The only reason that he punishes children was because they weren't honest to any people. Nancy immediately knew that the reason she always gets scolded is dishonest. The owner called them to leave. He also said he won’t punish them. Susan asked him if he needed help to clean the glass shards on the floor. The owner declined to let them help.

When Nancy arrived home, she thought, “What a meaningful day!”

A Basketball Incident

Last Tuesday after school, Nancy, Susan, and Peter were happily shooting hoops on the playground near Nancy’s house. The girls’ team was winning their friendly game, and Peter grew more and more excited. Without thinking, he hurled the ball—and none of them saw the accident coming.

Nancy and Susan could not stop it in time. The ball flew straight into a window at Nancy’s neighbour’s flat. Nancy’s stomach dropped: that neighbour was strict, and she was sure she would be blamed. Susan and Peter screamed, afraid of flying glass. For a moment, all three felt as if it would be the worst day of their lives.

Peter bolted, shouting over his shoulder that they were in serious trouble. Nancy and Susan stayed behind, minds racing over how to apologize to the owner. Everything felt chaotic.

Then heavy footsteps approached. When the man reached the broken window, he demanded, “Who did this? Tell me now.” The children stood frozen to the spot; no one wanted to be punished. Just then, Peter stepped forward and admitted that he had broken the glass.

Instead of scolding him, the neighbour said, “You are an honest child. I only punish young people when they are dishonest.” Nancy understood at once why she was often scolded: she had not always told the truth. He told them to go home and promised he would not punish them. Susan asked whether he wanted help sweeping up the shards, but he thanked her and said he would manage on his own.

When Nancy got home, she smiled to herself. “What a meaningful day,” she thought.
