Magazine article — should students share dark humour memes?

Some students send dark humour memes in group chats — skits or edits that joke about disabled or LGBTQ people. Staff and parents disagree on whether this should be allowed.

Write a magazine article on whether students should be allowed to send memes like these. State your view, give three reasons, and answer the other side in one short paragraph.

  • Only the yellow word groups are suggested edits; the rest is the student’s draft as written.
  • Your draft takes a ban view and has a short However paragraph that can grow into the “other side” section.

Draft length (student), about 115 words (well under a full magazine article; room to add a title, three clear reasons, group chat context, and LGBTQ).

Useful words (from the task) (English · 粵語) dark humour(黑色幽默), meme(迷因／梗圖), group chat(群組聊天), allow / ban(允許／禁止), offensive(冒犯), target(針對), disability(殘疾), LGBTQ(性小眾), bullying(欺凌), harm(傷害), respect(尊重), dignity(尊嚴), free speech(言論自由), school rule(校規), consequence(後果), in my view(我認為)

What went well

You already lean toward banning these memes and show awareness of harm.

  • 👍 Clear view. You argue students should not send dark humour memes.
  • 👍 Harm. You explain emotional hurt for people with disabilities and annoyance for parents and teachers.
  • 👍 Other side started. Paragraph 2 begins with However and gives a reason some students still share memes (stress, wanting fun).
  • 👍 Sensible close. You suggest other ways to feel happy instead of offensive jokes.

Could be even better

  • 💡 Task shape. Add a title, open with In my view, these memes should be banned, then three separate reasons (harm, bullying/respect, school rules/consequences).
  • 💡 Topic coverage. Mention group chats and LGBTQ people, not only disability, as the brief asks.
  • 💡 Other side. Keep paragraph 2 as one short counter-argument (e.g. stress, free speech) then answer it in your final line.
  • 💡 Language. People feel stressed, not stressful; use task words like offensive, target, dignity where they fit.

I think people shouldn’t send dark humour memes to others, like if disabled people see other people laughing at people who might have disabilities, they might make them feel very stressful and pessimistic and not willing to look forward to society. Other people like the sender's parents and teachers will also feel annoyed.

However, a lot of people still send dark humour memes to others for fun nowadays. The reason might be the world now is busy and it makes students or workers feel unhappy. They use it for making resting times more interesting and funny. I guess they should use another idea for making them happy then.

Comments

Your instinct matches the task direction (these memes cause harm and should not be shared). To meet the magazine article brief fully, separate three reasons, name group chats, and include LGBTQ alongside disability.
