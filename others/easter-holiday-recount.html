Easter holiday

A short recount of her Easter: dim sum with family in Tin Shui Wai, cycling, Pizza Hut, then home. Yellow highlights suggest clearer English—her story stays the same.

What went well

Clear sequence and warm family focus.

  • 🗓️ Structure — dim sum, cycling, Pizza Hut, then home: readers can follow the day.
  • 🍽️ Senses — “delicious,” “crispy,” “tasty” and photos give something to picture.
  • 🚲 Local detail — Tin Shui Wai and hiring bikes through an app feels real and specific.
  • 💭 Reflection — you end by naming quality time and treasure; that answers “why it mattered.”

Could be even better

  • 💡 Punctuation: use a comma, not a full stop, after time phrases such as “Yesterday morning” when the same sentence continues.
  • 💡 Spelling / articles: watch restaurant, lunch, uncle, and a / the before place names (e.g. a Chinese restaurant).
  • 💡 Word choice: bikes from an app are usually hired or rented, not “borrowed,” in formal English.
  • 💡 Brands: capitals for Pizza Hut; often we ate at Pizza Hut reads more naturally than “ate pizza Hut.”
  • 💡 Linking: avoid a lone but before “Finally;” use a time linker (Later, … / Afterwards, …) or start fresh with Finally.

5/4 = Things I did in Easter Holiday

I had a lot of memorable experiences in this Easter Holiday. It made me feel extremely thrilled!

Yesterday morning. my family and I went to Tin Shui Wai to Chinese restuarant to have hunch with my grandfather. We ordered some dim sum, for example, siu mai, ha gau. It was really delicious!

After that, we used our phones to borrow some bicycles to spend family time. We rode for around 2 hours to cycle whole of the Tin Shui Wai. Although it was tiring, still we enjoyed a lot and we took a lot of beautiful photos!

In a blink of an eye, it was already around 5p.m. My aunt and unele ate pizza Hut with us. The pizzas were crispy and tasty!

But finally, we got back home with tiring but happy feelings. Holiday is a time for family to spend quality time with each other so we need to treasure it!

Comments

You already have the ingredients of a strong magazine piece: a real day out, food, exercise, and a clear mood (tired but happy). Tightening grammar and spelling will let that voice come through without distracting the reader.

The yellow highlights are optional upgrades—your ideas stay yours. For publication, a simple line about why the cycling or the meal mattered (one extra sentence) would deepen the ending without making it long.
