Village speech: what to do — and what not to do

You are a doctor in a village during the Black Death. Villagers are scared. Write a short speech or letter in language they could understand (you may hint at isolation, cleanliness, rats or fleas in simple words). Say clearly: two things they should do to reduce the plague, and two things they should not do, with a short reason for each. Avoid modern textbook wording unless you explain it simply.

  • Audience: frightened villagers; tone of calm authority.
  • Only the yellow word groups are suggested edits; the rest is the student’s draft as written.

Draft length (student): about 263 words · about 1,390 characters · about 40 sentences · about 14 paragraphs (including list lines).

Useful words (from the task) (English · 粵語) plague / pestilence(瘟疫／鼠疫), quarantine / isolation(隔離), rumour / hearsay(謠言), hygiene(衞生), contagion(傳染), panic(恐慌), remedy(療法／藥方), calm authority(沉著權威)

What went well

The task shape is clear: symptoms, fear, two “do” rules, two “don’t” rules, and a practical close (call the doctor early, steady care).

  • 🎯 Content — cleaning, waste, fleas, separate bedding, bad smoke and potions, and not driving out the sick all match the brief.
  • 🗣️ Voice — short sentences and plain words suit a village crowd; the ending with God reads as period-appropriate.
  • 📏 Length — word count sits in a strong band for a classroom speech.

Could be even better

  • 💡 Sentences: fix a few fragments and run-ons (comma splices, broken lines after “they”).
  • 💡 Punctuation: watch double full stops (..) and add commas after short lead-ins (Today, … / If you are sick, …).
  • 💡 Word patterns: as much as you can needs as; make things worse needs things (or you).
  • 💡 Task vocabulary: one or two careful uses of pestilence, rumour, or remedy (with a plain gloss) could lift the register without sounding like a textbook.

Speech to the village (draft)

People of this village,

I am your doctor. I have seen how this sickness works. It starts with a fever. Makes you feel very weak. Then you get swelling in your groin or armpit. Many people die from it. You are scared. Fear makes you think of bad things.. We must be smart. We must do what helps and not make things worse.

Here are two things you should do:

  • Keep your homes clean. Do not let filth pile up. The dirt and waste attract. Small pests. They carry the sickness from one person to another.
  • Wash your skin when you can. Change clothes when possible. Do not share bedding and clothes. The sickness often spreads through fleas and dirty habits. Fresh washing and separate bedding can slow it down.

Here are two things you should not do:

  • Do not burn mixtures or strong-smelling smoke in every room. Do not drink potions made by people who promise quick fixes. Some mixtures can make the worse. Smoke does not fix the problem. It only makes your body weaker.
  • Do not blame your neighbours. Do not drive out the sick in anger. When people are chased away they. Do not get help. The sickness grows in secret. Fear makes more people sick.

If you feel well today keep yourself clean. If you are sick stay away from others much as you can. Let me come to you early. We cannot stop the sickness at once.. We can make it less bad, with simple steady care.

May God give us strength to do what is right.

Comments

The ideas are strong and humane: hygiene, separation of bedding, warnings against fake cures and mob fear, and an offer to visit the sick.
