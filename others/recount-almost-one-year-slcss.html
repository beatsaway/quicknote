ENFigurative language — Good instinct to open with a time idiom. The standard phrase is In the blink of an eye (with the, not a): it means something happens extremely quickly, suddenly, or almost instantaneously. Native speakers treat In a blink of an eye as grammatically wrong. After the idiom, use a comma, not a full stop, so the sentence flows: In the blink of an eye, I have been … You can also add one or two similes or metaphors at key Sports Day moments to make scenes more vivid, without piling on fancy words.

粵修辭建議 — 開首用時間慣用語，方向啱。標準講法係 In the blink of an eye（用 the，唔好用 a），意思係事情發生得好快、好突然；母語者會當 In a blink of an eye 係文法錯。慣用語後面用逗號而唔係句號，句子先順：In the blink of an eye, I have been … 另可喺運動會關鍵場面加一至兩個比喻／明喻，令畫面更立體，又唔使堆砌華麗詞藻。

ENExamples
• Qualifying finish: “I shot across the line like an arrow.” / “My heart thumped like a drum.”
• Relay start: “At the beep, our class burst forward like arrows from a bow.”
• House cheering: “Four houses’ shouts rolled together like waves hitting the shore.”
• After the medal: “The silver medal felt cold in my palm, but my chest felt warm.”

粵例子
• 預賽衝線：「我好似一支箭咁衝過終點。」／「心臟撲通撲通，像鼓聲一樣快。」
• 接力開跑：「嗶一聲，全班像離弦之箭衝出去。」
• 打氣台：「四社嘅吶喊匯成一片聲浪，像潮水拍岸。」
• 頒獎後：「銀牌喺手心冷冷地，心裡卻暖烘烘。」
