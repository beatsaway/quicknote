Creative writing — one trip back as a first-class passenger

Imagine you time-travel once to the Titanic in April 1912 as a first-class passenger. No impossible gadgets—only what fits the era. You remember what happened that night. Write a short diary, speech, or reflection. Name three specific things you would try to do to save more people (before and/or after the iceberg). After each, explain why it might help. Stay plausible for 1912.

Diary / reflection (draft)

April 14, 1912. The air is very still on A-Deck, the ocean calm. I am waiting for 11:40 p.m. And I am getting more and more nervous. I know what the Titanic is going to do. As a first-class passenger on the Titanic I have no tools but want to try to save people.

First I want to get the ten forty P. M. Bridge ice advisory. I'll go down to the Marconi wireless room and make sure the captain gets the word about the ice ahead. Maybe the captain can change the direction of the ship.

Next time we are being evacuated from the ship I will find Second Officer Lightoller. I want to make sure the lifeboats are filled with as many people as they can carry. Each lifeboat can safely hold 65 people. If we fill them up we can save 468 more lives.

Then I'll use my resources to get the stewards to open the gates to the steerage area. This will assist the third class passengers to reach the boat deck faster. The beautiful stars in the sky are a reminder of the many lives that are at risk. I must seek Captain Smith elsewhere. The Titanic is in trouble and I want to help save the people on the Titanic.

Comments

Strong structure: three survival steps with motives, plus atmosphere. Tighten a few phrases so time-travel is singular (“when evacuation begins”), sharpen the wireless / bridge idea, and smooth the last paragraph so Captain Smith and the stars feel part of one plan.
