Letter of advice and encouragement for Amy

Amy feels down, lonely, and worried about poor exam results. Write a letter of advice and encouragement as Michelle. Cover problems Amy faces, how she can overcome them, how you encourage her, and anything else helpful. Only the yellow word groups are suggested edits; the rest is the student’s draft as written.

  • Tone should be warm, supportive, and practical, not blaming.
  • Structure in the draft moves from opening to friends, exams, encouragement, and an offer to help again.

Draft length (student), about 250 words · 4 body paragraphs · sign-off Best wishes, Michelle

Useful words (from the task) (English · 粵語) discouraged(氣餒), encouragement(鼓勵), overcome(克服), extracurricular(課外活動), participate(參與), struggle(掙扎／感到困難), seek help(尋求協助), pay attention(專心), self-regulation(自律), stand by someone(支持某人)

What went well

You answer the task with a caring tone and clear sections.

  • 👍 Task coverage. Few friends, bad exams, and “you’re not alone” encouragement are all there.
  • 👍 Practical ideas. Clubs (dance, chess, band), asking teachers/seniors, and paying attention in class are realistic.
  • 👍 Structure. First of all / Secondly / Last but not least helps Amy follow your advice.
  • 👍 Ending. You invite her to write back; that keeps the friendship warm.

Could be even better

  • 💡 Open with empathy. Add one sentence that echoes Amy’s feelings (I’m sorry you’ve been feeling down) before giving tips.
  • 💡 Grammar. Watch few time, exam results are, participating, your difficulties, and looking forward to.
  • 💡 Broken sentences. Tighten the long opening about friends and the line after struggled (missing link word / full stop).
  • 💡 Word choice. it’s shy to ask → you might feel shy or there’s nothing to be ashamed of.
  • 💡 Spelling. extracurricular activities (one word, double c).
  • 💡 Richer advice. Group tips by benefit (health / study / fun). See bilingual ideas in Comments.

Dear Amy,

It seems like you’re feeling a bit discouraged in this few time. Here are my some suggestions to help you solve your problems.

First of all, it seems like less friends that you can talk to is one of your stress that you are facing. I suggest you can be more outgoing by participanting more extra-curricular activties, for example, dance club, chess club and school band. Therefore you can have some friends who have same interests with you so you can have topic to chat with

Secondly, I understand that your exam results is one of the thing that makes you struggled That is my suggestion, you can ask me, senior students or even teachers! Don't think that it's shy to ask them. It's very normal that everyone can face difficulties during studying. However, paying attention in class is also important for you to get better results in your exam because teachers may just quickly say the exam key points during the class. If you're not paying enough attention, you may miss a lot of important key points.

Last but not least, Amy, you’re not alone. I will always stand by your side to encouraging you. I hope these suggestions can help you get rid of you difficulties. If you further difficulties that you're facing and can't get rid of them. Feel free to ask me and I'll give some suggestions! I'm always looking forward for your reply!

Best wishes,
Michelle

Comments

Michelle sounds like a real friend. She offers concrete steps and ends with support. Tightening grammar, spelling, and a few long sentences will let that warmth come through clearly. Yellow spans are optional upgrades. Click to compare.

For a stronger letter, add one empathic sentence at the start (acknowledge Amy’s letter) and, if you can, one small study habit (e.g. a weekly revision plan) so the exam advice feels even more actionable.

Letter to Amy (demo)

Dear Amy,

I was sorry to hear that you have been feeling so low. Feeling down, lonely, and worried about your exams is hard, but it does not mean you are failing at everything. I have thought about what might help, and I would like to share a few ideas.

First, about friends. Not having many people to talk to can be one of your greatest worries. For your health, you could play basketball or badminton after school. You would meet classmates in a relaxed way, and exercise would help you stay physically fit and mentally alert. You could still join the dance club, the chess club, or the school band if you would like to. The important thing is to meet people through something you enjoy.

Regarding your education, disappointing exam results are painful, but they can improve. Make friends in the library and study together. Share notes, test each other, and practise speaking English together. Do not be afraid to ask me, older students, or teachers for help. It is normal to need support. Also pay close attention in lessons. Teachers sometimes mention important exam points quickly, and you do not want to miss them.

Finally, remember fun. Take up a hobby at the art club, such as a manga-drawing workshop, and allow yourself to enjoy it. Do not let exams fill your whole life. Relax and recharge from time to time. Having fun is part of being a teenager, and you deserve to live like one.

Amy, you are not alone. I will always stand by you and encourage you. If you still face difficulties, please write to me at any time. I look forward to hearing from you.

Best wishes,
Michelle
