What to improve (from your writing so far)

  • Argument essays. Give two or three reasons, not three copies of the same story. Vary your examples (one success, one small step, one lesson learned).
  • Past examples. When you tell a personal story, use past tense and would for feelings in the past (I was afraid I would forget).
  • Advice letters. Start with empathy, then give 2–3 clear steps, then encourage. Use the right sign-off (Yours sincerely if you know the name).
  • One idea per sentence. Especially in introductions and conclusions. Short sentences are fine.

Shared toolkit (Level 5+)

Signposting (argument)
  • First and foremost, …
  • In addition, … / Another point is that …
  • Moreover, … / What is more, …
  • Nevertheless, … / Admittedly, …
  • In conclusion, … / To sum up, …
Opinion openers
  • I am firmly convinced that …
  • There is no doubt that …
  • It is imperative that …
  • From my perspective, …
Cause & effect
  • This may give rise to …
  • Consequently, … / As a result, …
  • This can undermine … / This fosters …
  • For instance, … / A case in point is …
Advice letter tone
  • Thank you for sharing your problem.
  • I can understand why you feel overwhelmed.
  • To begin with, I would strongly encourage you to …
  • It is advisable to … / A viable option is to …
  • Remember that … I wish you all the best.

Grammar reminders (check every time)

After help, use base verb. It helps us grow, not helps us growing.
No double comparatives. stronger or more confident, not more stronger.
Past story. I was afraid I would fail, not I will fail.
Full of. full of problems, not full with problems.

Argument writing tasks

Aim for about 350–450 words. State your opinion in paragraph 1. Give at least two reasons with examples. Use at least 4 Level 5+ words from each task list.

Task A1. Should schools ban smartphones in class?

Essay · agree or disagree · school life

Some teachers want a full ban on smartphones during lessons. Others say students should learn to use phones responsibly. Do you agree that smartphones should be banned in class? Explain your view with reasons and examples.
Level 5+ vocab English · 粵語. Try to use at least 4 in your draft. detrimental(有害), undermine(削弱), impose(實施), foster(培養), mitigate(減輕), stringent(嚴格), cognitive engagement(認知投入), legitimate concern(合理顧慮)
Intro
  • I am firmly convinced that smartphones should / should not be banned in class because …
  • Although phones are useful, constant notifications can undermine learning during lessons.
Reason 1 + example
  • First and foremost, phones are detrimental to concentration. When students check messages, they lose cognitive engagement with the lesson.
  • For instance, in my Form 4 class, I once lost track of a maths explanation because I glanced at my phone.
Reason 2 + example
  • In addition, a stringent ban can foster self-discipline.
  • When our school imposed phone-free lessons for one month, many classmates participated more actively in discussions.
Other view + conclusion
  • Admittedly, some people raise the legitimate concern that students need phones for emergencies. Nevertheless, devices can remain switched off in bags.
  • In conclusion, a clear policy would mitigate distraction and protect learning.
Quick plan Use the timer above. Opinion: … Reason 1: … Example: … Reason 2: … Example: … Other view: …

Task A2. Is part-time work good for senior students?

Essay · pros and cons · social issues

More Hong Kong students take part-time jobs. Some parents worry it hurts exam results. Others think it prepares young people for adult life. Do you think part-time work is a good idea for senior secondary students?
Level 5+ vocab English · 粵語. Try to use at least 4 in your draft. strike a balance(取得平衡), cultivate(培養), financial autonomy(財務自主), academic pursuits(學業追求), instil(灌輸), viable(可行的), alleviate(減輕), detrimental(不利的)
Intro
  • From my perspective, part-time work can be beneficial for senior students if they strike a balance between earning and studying.
Reason 1
  • First of all, a part-time job cultivates time management. Students learn to finish homework before a shift.
  • My cousin works in a café on Saturdays. She plans her revision carefully so her academic pursuits do not suffer.
Reason 2
  • Another point is that students gain financial autonomy and real-world confidence by serving customers.
Limit + conclusion
  • Nevertheless, excessive hours can be detrimental. Schools and parents should set a viable limit, such as eight hours a week.
  • To sum up, part-time work is worthwhile when it instils responsibility without overwhelming students.

Task A3. Learning from failure (quote essay)

Essay · quote + personal examples · like your fear essay

‘Failure is the mother of success.’ How important is it to learn from failure? Write an essay explaining your opinion with relevant examples from your personal experience.
Level 5+ vocab English · 粵語. Try to use at least 4 in your draft. setback(挫折), resilience(韌性), perseverance(堅毅), reflect critically(深入反思), transformative(轉變性的), invaluable(極寶貴的), adversity(逆境), bounce back(東山再起)
Intro
  • The saying ‘Failure is the mother of success’ suggests that setbacks can be invaluable teachers.
  • I am firmly convinced that learning from failure is essential rather than giving up.
Body (vary the endings)
  • First and foremost, every setback reveals a weak point. When I failed a maths test, I began to reflect critically on my habits.
  • In addition, adversity builds perseverance. I did not win my first debate, but I prepared stronger arguments next time.
  • Moreover, even a small improvement counts. After losing a match, our team trained harder and bounced back with a better score, although we still lost.
Conclusion
  • In conclusion, failure is not the end. It can be a transformative step towards growth.
  • As the quote suggests, resilience often comes after we learn from what went wrong.

Letters of advice

Aim for about 350–400 words. Warm tone. At least 2–3 pieces of advice with reasons. Use at least 4 Level 5+ words per letter.

Task B1. Friend stressed about DSE English Paper 2

Advice letter · Teen magazine column

I am in Form 6 and DSE is coming. English Paper 2 terrifies me. I never know which text type to choose, and I run out of time. I feel like giving up. What should I do?

— Alex, 17

Level 5+ vocab English · 粵語. Try to use at least 4 in your draft. overwhelmed(難以承受), daunting(令人望而生畏), alleviate(減輕), viable(可行的), structured outline(有結構大綱), demystify(使不再神秘), empathetic(富同理心), proactive(積極的)
Open + empathise
  • Dear Alex,
  • Thank you for your letter. I can understand why DSE English Paper 2 feels daunting.
  • Many Form 6 students feel overwhelmed when choosing a question, so you are not alone.
Advice 1–3
  • To begin with, spend five minutes reading every question and pick the text type you can answer most fully.
  • In addition, always write a structured outline before you start. This will help you because examiners can follow your ideas more easily.
  • Another useful step is to practise one past paper each week under timed conditions. This can demystify the exam and make it feel more manageable.
Close
  • Take proactive steps each week, and improvement will follow. I wish you all the best.
  • Yours sincerely, …

Task B2. Cousin wants to quit the swim team

Advice letter · personal letter

Your cousin Sam writes to you. Sam loves swimming but the coach is strict, training is exhausting, and Sam wants to quit. Write a letter giving advice.
Level 5+ vocab English · 粵語. Try to use at least 4 in your draft. exhausted(筋疲力盡), burnout(過勞), communicate openly(坦誠溝通), alleviate(減輕), reignite(重新點燃), sustainable(可持續的), reassure(令…安心), deliberate(審慎的)
Open
  • Dear Sam,
  • I was sorry to hear that training has been so exhausting lately.
  • It is completely normal to feel overwhelmed when a coach applies intense pressure before competitions.
Advice
  • First and foremost, before you quit, I would strongly encourage you to communicate openly with your coach about your workload.
  • In addition, consider taking a short break so you can alleviate stress and avoid burnout.
  • If you still want to leave after a month, make a deliberate decision rather than quitting in anger.
Close
  • I reassure you that your passion for swimming may still be there. Give yourself time to find a sustainable balance.
  • Write back anytime. Yours sincerely, …

Letters of suggestion

Aim for about 300–400 words. Describe a problem, propose an idea, explain benefits. Polite but clear tone.

Task C1. Suggest a study-skills workshop

Letter to principal · formal suggestion

Write to your principal. Suggest the school organise a study-skills workshop for Form 4–6 students before the mock exams. Explain why it is needed and what the workshop should include.
Level 5+ vocab English · 粵語. Try to use at least 4 in your draft. implement(實施), feasible(可行的), holistic(全面的), tangible(具體的), alleviate(減輕), prioritise(優先), contemplate(考慮), equip(裝備)
Open + problem
  • Dear Principal,
  • I am writing to propose a study-skills workshop that could equip senior students with more effective revision methods.
  • Many classmates study for long hours yet still feel unprepared for mock exams because they lack a holistic strategy.
Proposal + benefits
  • First of all, a feasible two-hour workshop could cover note-taking, time management and how to analyse past papers.
  • In addition, inviting an alumni speaker would make the session practical rather than theoretical.
  • This would bring tangible benefits, alleviate exam stress and help students prioritise their weak areas.
Close
  • The Student Union would be happy to help implement the event if needed.
  • I hope you will contemplate my suggestion. Yours sincerely, …

Task C2. Suggest a peer-mentoring scheme

Letter to school board / Student Union · suggestion

Some Form 1 students struggle to adapt to secondary school. Suggest a peer-mentoring scheme where senior students support junior students. Write a letter explaining your idea.
Level 5+ vocab English · 粵語. Try to use at least 4 in your draft. facilitate(促進), foster(培養), inclusive(共融的), alleviate(減輕), cultivate(培養), sense of belonging(歸屬感), mentorship(導師制), transition(過渡)
Structure
  • I am writing to propose a peer-mentoring scheme that would facilitate Form 1 students’ transition to secondary school.
  • Many newcomers feel isolated and unsure how to manage homework and clubs.
  • Another point is that pairing each Form 1 class with Form 4 mentors could foster a stronger sense of belonging.
  • Seniors would cultivate leadership while juniors receive guidance in an inclusive school culture.
  • I hope the school will contemplate this plan. Yours sincerely, …
