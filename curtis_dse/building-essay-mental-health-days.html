HKDSE English Language — Paper 2 Part B

Question — Mental health days as excused absences

Situation

After several schools reported rising counselling referrals, a parent group proposes that secondary students be allowed a small number of 'mental health days' each year as excused absences, without a medical certificate for every case. Some principals fear misuse; others say the policy would reduce crisis absences later.

Task

Write an argumentative essay for the PTA newsletter. Should secondary schools allow a limited number of mental health days as excused absences? Give reasons and examples to support your view.

  • Write about 500 words.
  • State your view clearly, support it with reasons and examples, and deal with the other side.

Should secondary schools allow limited mental health days as excused absences?

Student wellbeing is now a frequent school agenda item. While some fear misuse of leave, limited mental health days could prevent sharper crises. In my view, secondary schools should allow a small number of mental health days as excused absences with clear limits and follow-up.

First and foremost, limited leave can stop stress from becoming a longer collapse. For example, a student who takes one planned recovery day may return able to face tests instead of disappearing for a week. As a result, attendance patterns can become more honest and manageable.

Secondly, a formal policy can also reduce stigma around asking for help. By naming mental health leave openly, schools may discourage silent struggling until exam week. In turn, more students seek counselling earlier.

Critics may argue that students will fake stress to skip tests. However, a yearly cap, form-teacher check-ins and catch-up plans can limit misuse while still helping genuine cases. Therefore, a careful mental-health-day policy remains worthwhile.

In conclusion, limited mental health days can prevent longer absences if caps and follow-up exist. While misuse concerns are valid, a careful policy is still justified.
