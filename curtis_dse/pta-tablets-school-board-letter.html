The task

School context / formal letter

Your school is considering replacing physical textbooks with tablets for all students next year.

You are the president of the parent-teacher association. Write a letter to the school board expressing the concerns of parents regarding screen time and digital distractions while also acknowledging the potential benefits.

Marking (CLO)

HKDSE Paper 2 Part B is assessed on Content, Language and Organisation. The levels below are indicative only for study — not official HKEAA marks.

Content — Level 4

  • Role and audience — PTA president to the school board; concerns + benefits both appear (balanced brief).
  • Parents’ worries — screen time (health), distractions (games/social media), and concrete asks (guidelines, filtering, rules).
  • Benefits — updated materials, interactivity, learning quality; links to “careful planning.”
  • To push higher: name one specific safeguard (e.g. MDM, homework-only profiles) in a single sentence.

Language — Level 3

  • Ideas are clear; articles, prepositions and word form slip often (access to, impact on, content).
  • Salutation should match a board letter (Dear Members of the School Board).
  • Capital letters for proper nouns (Google, YouTube, Instagram).

Organisation — Level 4

  • Logical flow: introduction → screen time → distractions → benefits → closing.
  • Cohesion works (First of all / Also / We understand).
  • Minor repetition (need, rules) — synonyms would tighten Level 5.

How to do better next time

  • Register: Open with the correct salutation and sign off with Parent-Teacher Association capped up.
  • Accuracy: Proofread for collocations (have an impact on, make it hard for someone to) and countability (content vs contents).
  • Consistency: Say tablets throughout if that is the school plan, rather than mixing in iPad unless the policy names one device.
  • Tone: Keep concern firm but polite; pair “punishments” with clear expectations so it sounds constructive.

Overall (indicative): about Level 3–4 — strong task fit and structure; language accuracy holds the band.

Dear school members,

As the president of the parent-teacher association, I am writing to focus on the concern about the plan of replacing traditional physical textbooks with tablets for all students starting next year. Although this plan can surely bring benefits to both students and teachers, we need to be careful in considering some challenges that may happen and we need to strike a balance between students behaviour and academic.

First of all, one of the main concerns among parents is the increasing screen time in using tablets. Students need to focus on their iPad for a long time every day which will cause health issues including short sight, headaches, bad sleep quality and reducing of physical activity. Our association believes that without proper management and monitoring systems, students may spend longer time staring at screens each day, which could lead to negative impact in their eyes and mental health. We hope the school department can list a guideline about screen time and promote breaks during lessons to reduce these risks.

Also, digital devices can be a distraction for students. There are different ways to access to online games and social media. For example, students can use google to search YouTube, instagram and subway surfer website versions and use them. These non-educational contents may cause students hard to stay focused on classes. This phenomenon is significant in the young students who have no mature self-regulation systems. We recommend that the IT department in school should install block system in every device which can block the access to other non-educational contents. Schools should also set rules and punishments for students who improperly using their devices.

We understand that this plan can bring benefits to students. It can provide updated information and educational resources to students and make lessons more interactive that can enhance the quality of learning. However, this policy needs some rules to ensure that students can use their digital devices wisely and effectively. We believe that careful planning and rules can make tablets be a powerful tool for students to learn. We appreciate the school commitment to improve the quality of education and we look forward to working together to ensure that this policy can benefit students.

Yours sincerely,
Chris Wong
President of the parent teacher association

Dear Members of the School Board, I am writing as President of the Parent-Teacher Association to share parents’ views on the proposal to replace physical textbooks with tablets for all students next year. Parents recognise possible benefits: fresher materials, richer resources and more interactive lessons can support teaching and learning. Many families nevertheless worry about two areas in particular. First, extended daily screen use may affect students’ health—eyesight, sleep and opportunities for physical activity—unless the school caps instructional screen time, adjusts fonts and brightness, and builds in movement breaks. Second, tablets open easy routes to games and social media. Younger students especially may struggle with self-control. We urge the school to deploy central filtering or mobile device management, clear usage rules, proportionate consequences for misuse, and ongoing education on responsible device habits. With transparent guidelines and safeguards, tablets can be powerful tools rather than sources of distraction. We appreciate the board’s commitment to raising educational quality and stand ready to work with staff on implementation that protects wellbeing as well as learning. Yours sincerely, Chris Wong President of the Parent-Teacher Association
