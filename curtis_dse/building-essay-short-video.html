Situation

Task

Should secondary schools teach students how to limit short-form video use?

Short-form video now fills many students' free time. While families should guide screen use, schools see the effects on attention and sleep. In my view, secondary schools should teach students how to limit short-form video use through practical workshops, not scare campaigns.

First and foremost, school guidance can protect study focus. For example, students who set app timers before revision often finish homework with fewer breaks. As a result, evening attention becomes steadier for public-exam practice.

Secondly, workshops can also build healthier sleep routines. By teaching a no-phone wind-down, schools may discourage late-night autoplay loops. In turn, more students arrive ready for morning lessons.

Critics may argue that screen habits belong only to parents. However, schools already teach health and study skills, so short attention workshops can support families rather than replace them. Therefore, practical workshops remain worthwhile.

In conclusion, schools can protect focus and sleep with practical workshops. While family roles are valid, school guidance can support better habits without scare tactics.
