E. Short writing

Write your own answer first. Click the grey box to see a model.

  1. Write 3 sentences arguing for food warning labels. Use First of all / For example / Therefore.

    Click to reveal a model answer

    First of all, warning labels give shoppers a quick signal about sugar, salt or fat. For example, a red “high in sugar” mark on sweets can make people rethink the purchase. Therefore, labels can help change everyday eating habits.

  2. Write 2 sentences for the opposite view, then 1 rebuttal starting with However.

    Click to reveal a model answer

    Some critics say customers simply ignore warning labels, so habits do not change. Labels alone cannot fix obesity without education and pricing policies. However, labels are still useful because they remind shoppers at the moment of buying and work best together with other measures.

  3. Rewrite more naturally: “It can lead to a better future when customers see high in sugar.”

    Click to reveal a model answer

    When customers see a “high in sugar” warning, they may rethink the purchase and choose a healthier option.

    Click for sample points

  2. Give one example of a label you have seen.

    Click for sample points

    Example: a red “high in sugar” / “high in salt” mark on a soft drink or snack pack; traffic-light labels on packaging.

  3. What else (besides labels) can change eating habits?

    Click for sample points

    Health education at school; higher tax on sugary drinks; cheaper healthy options; family cooking habits; PE / sport; clearer menus in canteens.
