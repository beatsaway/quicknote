The task

School context / formal letter

The principal is considering a ban on smartphones during school hours and lunch breaks. As Chris Wong, Chairperson of the Student Union, you were asked to survey students and write a formal letter reporting their views.

Include: reasons some students support a ban (distraction, privacy/safety); reasons others oppose or worry (resentment, social needs); and a clear recommendation that is balanced (e.g. phone-free zones, responsible use, education) rather than only “ban everything” or “no rules.”

Marking (CLO)

HKDSE Paper 2 Part B is assessed on Content, Language and Organisation. The levels below are indicative only for study — not official HKEAA marks.

Content — Level 4

  • Role and audience — Student Union chair to the principal; survey duty stated up front.
  • Range of views — distraction, privacy, resentment, lunch-time social use, and phone-free zones all appear.
  • Recommendation — balanced regulation (zones + education) rather than a blunt full ban.
  • To push higher: one crisp survey statistic (e.g. “62% supported …”) would strengthen the report tone.

Language — Level 3

  • Ideas are clear; watch articles (a ban on), plurals (smartphones), and word form (reducing, perpetuate).
  • Salutation — Dear Principal, with a capital letter.
  • Possessives — students’ needs.

Organisation — Level 4

  • Flow: purpose → pros of ban → cons → social angle → PTA/zones → recommendation → close.
  • Linkers work (However / When it comes to / Furthermore / Instead of).
  • Ending ties back to self-regulation and school atmosphere.

How to do better next time

  • Report tone: Briefly say how many students answered or one headline result from the survey.
  • Accuracy: Avoid double verbs (might perpetuate, not might cause perpetuate).
  • Consistency: Use phones or smartphones steadily; hyphenate phone-free zones.
  • Register: Keep formal closings: Yours sincerely, then name and title.

Overall (indicative): about Level 3–4 — strong balance and recommendation; language accuracy holds the band.

Dear principal,

As requested, I have set up a survey to students to show their opinions about the ban of smartphones during school hours and lunch breaks. I think it is important to consider before adding to the school rules.

Many students agree that smartphone is a distraction in class. Some students overshare location details and photos during school hours which cause privacy and safety concerns. However, others feel that adding a ban might cause perpetuate resentment rather than solving distraction. They believe that such measure could lead to resistance and anger among students.

When it comes to social interaction, students said that lunch break phone use often affects face-to-face social communication. While some see the benefit of reduce using phone during breaks, many also expressed concerns that banning phone could bring anxiety to students who rely on their phones for social connection and entertainment.

Furthermore, students suggest that the PTA should carefully evaluate a ban or a focus on responsible use of phone which can bring a better school atmosphere. Many students propose curating specific phone-free zones, which can strike a balance by allowing students to manage their phone responsibly without feeling overly pressured.

In these opinions, I recommend that the school should consider a better regulation. Instead of forcing students to stop using mobile phones, setting a phone-free zones can ease the distraction and risks and also respecting students needs. This can encourage students to have better self-regulation and promote a healthier balance between phones and themselves. A mix of education, better guidelines, and special zones can foster a more happy and respectful school environment.

Yours sincerely,
Chris Wong
Chairperson, Student Union

Dear Principal, As requested, I have conducted a survey among students on their views about a proposed ban on smartphones during school hours and lunch breaks. I believe these findings should be considered carefully before the rule is added. Many respondents agree that smartphones distract students in class. Some also overshare location details and photos during school hours, which raises privacy and safety concerns. However, others fear that a blanket ban might perpetuate resentment rather than solve distraction. They believe such measure could provoke resistance and anger. Regarding social interaction, students reported that phone use at lunch often reduces face-to-face conversation. While some welcome less phone use during breaks, many worry that banning phones would increase anxiety among students who depend on them for social connection and relaxation. Students further suggest that the PTA should weigh a full ban against promoting responsible phone use to improve school atmosphere. Many favour designated phone-free zones, striking a balance between management and trust. Based on these findings, I recommend measured regulation rather than an outright prohibition. Instead of forcing students to abandon mobile phones, the school could designate phone-free zones to limit distraction and risk while respecting students’ needs. Combined with clear guidelines and digital citizenship education, this approach can support self-regulation and a happier, more respectful campus. Yours sincerely, Chris Wong Chairperson, Student Union
