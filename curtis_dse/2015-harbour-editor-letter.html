The task

Learning English through Debating

Many parts of Hong Kong’s harbour front are still undeveloped. Some suggest using these areas for housing and commercial use, while others argue that such areas should be left as open space for public use. Write to the editor of the Hong Kong Daily stating your opinion either for or against leaving these areas open for public use. Justify your point of view with three reasons.

Marking (CLO)

HKDSE Paper 2 Part B is assessed on Content (ideas, relevance, task fulfilment), Language (accuracy, range, tone/register) and Organisation (structure, cohesion, paragraphing). The levels below are indicative only for study — not official HKEAA marks.

Content — Level 4

  • Takes a clear side (for keeping the harbour open) and gives three distinct reasons, as required.
  • Ideas — wellbeing, space for families, tourism/economy — are relevant to Hong Kong.
  • One body paragraph is slightly muddled: it sounds like “building” open space rather than not developing the waterfront — tighten the logic.
  • Could add brief local specifics (e.g. promenade use, skyline) to move towards Level 5.

Language — Level 3

  • Meaning is usually clear, but grammar and word choice slip often (agreement, prepositions, wrong words).
  • Some sentences are clunky; vocabulary is adequate but not yet wide or precise enough for Level 4.
  • Register is mostly appropriate for a letter to the editor; closing is correct.

Organisation — Level 4

  • Clear introduction, sequenced reasons (First / Also / Furthermore) and a short conclusion.
  • Paragraphing works; one short paragraph could merge with the next for smoother development.
  • Basic cohesion is present; more varied links would match Level 5.

How to do better next time

  • Content: Open each body paragraph with a clear topic sentence that names one reason only; for the environment point, frame it explicitly as avoiding overbuilding on the shore, not “creating” open space from nothing.
  • Content: Add one or two Hong Kong-focused details (e.g. crowded districts vs. need for breathing room, harbour as a postcard image) so arguments feel grounded, not generic.
  • Language: After drafting, do a pass only for agreement, articles and prepositions (e.g. along the harbour, the noise, visitors). Keep a personal error list from the yellow highlights and revise new essays against it.
  • Language: Replace loose word choices with set collocations (fast-paced city, green/open space, boost the economy) — short vocabulary notes help more than rare “fancy” words.
  • Organisation: Vary connectors beyond First / Also / Furthermore (In addition, Moreover, Above all) and use one sentence at the end of the intro to preview your three reasons.

Overall (indicative): about Level 3–4 — content and organisation outpace language; accuracy limits the overall band.

Dear Editor,

In recent years, there are more undeveloped harbour in Hong Kong. I am writing to share my opinion about the undeveloped areas. I believe that these areas should be left open for public use. There are three main reasons to support my view.

First, pointing open space along the harbour can give space for people to relax and enjoy the view of the sea. Hong Kong is a first pace city. Everyone has a busy life and they all work very hard. They need a phone to escape those noise and lights. Open areas of harbour can provide fresh air and a perfect environment for people. They can walk near the sea, sit on the benches or have picnics with friends or families in there.

Open spaces also give children a safe place to play and run around. If the areas are turned into buildings, there will be less place for recreational use.

Also, building an open space in the harbour can help protect the environment as sea is the habitat of fish and other sea creatures. If people develop the land into housing or commercial areas, this may harm the wildlife. The construction may also cause pollution and structure damage of land. Therefore, keeping the harbour open can maintain the natural environment of Hong Kong which is not only good for the animals but also benefit for people who lives nature.

Furthermore, open spaces along the harbour can boost tourism and even can boost the economy. Many tourists visit Hong Kong always want to find a place to enjoy the view and buildings. Walking paths and seats in harbour can make the open space more attractive. Tourists can take photos and having a walk in there. This can attract more vistors to Hong Kong and also more people spending money on the local shops nearby the harbour.

In conclusion, I strongly believe that the undeveloped parts of Hong Kong harbour should be open for public use. I think we should think carefully about how we use our land. Sometimes having open spaces for people is better than having housing or commercial areas, as open spaces are good for people health, environment and economy.

Yours sincerely,
Chris Wong

Dear Editor, Much of Hong Kong’s harbour front is still undeveloped. I am writing to argue that these stretches should stay open for public use rather than be turned mainly into housing or offices. I have three reasons for this view. First, a clear waterfront gives residents room to relax and enjoy the sea. Hong Kong is a fast-paced city where many people work long hours; they need places to escape the noise and glare of the urban core. Wide promenades offer fresher air and a calmer setting for walking, sitting on benches or picnicking with family and friends. Open recreational space also matters for children. If every spare strip is built over, families lose safe outdoor areas within easy reach. Second, limiting hard development along sensitive shoreline protects marine habitats. Heavy construction may disturb wildlife, add pollution and damage fragile coastal land. Keeping the harbour more natural supports biodiversity and benefits residents who value nature in the city. Third, an attractive harbour lifts tourism and the wider economy. Visitors often seek classic harbour views; thoughtful paths and seating make the experience more welcoming. That can draw more visitors and spending at shops and cafés nearby, supporting local employment. In short, undeveloped harbour frontage should remain publicly accessible. Used wisely, open space improves health, safeguards the environment and strengthens the economy in ways that relentless building alone cannot. Yours sincerely, Chris Wong
