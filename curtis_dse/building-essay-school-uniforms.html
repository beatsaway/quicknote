Should secondary schools require uniforms?

Uniforms are common in Hong Kong schools. While some want freer dress, uniforms can support equality and focus. In my view, secondary schools should continue to require uniforms.

First and foremost, uniforms reduce visible pressure to wear costly brands. For example, students compete less over logos and latest styles. As a result, the school day feels fairer for more families.

Secondly, a shared dress code can support a learning focus. By wearing the same set of clothes, schools may discourage morning fashion stress. In turn, students arrive ready for lessons, not outfit contests.

Critics may argue that uniforms limit self-expression. However, students can still show personality through clubs, projects and non-uniform days. Therefore, keeping uniforms is still justified.

In conclusion, uniforms support fairness and focus. While self-expression concerns are valid, clubs and non-uniform days can balance them.
