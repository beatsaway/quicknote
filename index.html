🔄 IB Design Cycle (ESSENTIAL)

The backbone of IB DT - you MUST know this:
1. Inquiring and Analyzing - Research and problem identification
2. Developing Ideas - Brainstorming and concept development
3. Creating the Solution - Prototyping and making
4. Evaluating - Testing and reflection

Your connection: "This mirrors exactly the iterative design process I used in 12 years of game development - research, ideation, prototyping, user testing, refinement."

❓ Key Interview Q&A

Q: "How familiar are you with IB assessment criteria?"
A: "I understand the IA contributes 40% of the final grade using specific criteria including research, development, and evaluation. The new 2025 curriculum emphasizes redesigning existing products, which suits my approach of analyzing current solutions before creating improvements. My game industry background helps students understand real-world design constraints."
Q: "Why move from London to Hong Kong?"
A: "Hong Kong is where I completed my undergraduate studies, so it feels like coming home. I'm excited about working in an international school environment with global perspectives. The opportunity to work with diverse students while applying my game design and interactive technology experience is exactly the career growth I'm seeking."
Q: "How would you support students through the design project?"
A: "I'd scaffold using the IB Design Cycle - thorough user research and problem analysis, then iterative prototyping. From game development, I know how to help students balance ambitious ideas with practical constraints. I'd use digital portfolios to document their design thinking with photos, sketches, and reflections."

🚫 Red Flags to Avoid

• Don't mention PhD discontinuation negatively
• Don't oversell unfamiliar areas (materials/textiles)
• Don't criticize current school
• Don't admit knowing nothing about IB DT
• Don't focus only on technology - emphasize design thinking
